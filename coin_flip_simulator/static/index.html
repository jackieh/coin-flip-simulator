Is the coin flip worth it?

Heads you win $1,050, tails you lose $1,000. On average that's +$25 per flip. But you're out of the game as soon as you can't cover a loss. This page works out exactly how often that happens, and what the bet is really worth at different starting bankrolls.

How this is calculated

  • Going broke means your wealth falls below the loss amount, so you can't cover the next tails. You stop and keep whatever's left.
  • After n flips with h heads your wealth is start + win·h − loss·(n−h). That's only n+1 possible states, so every probability here (except the random sample paths) is exact, computed by dynamic programming rather than estimated by simulation.
  • Going broke if you never stop uses the fact that e−R·W stays constant on average from flip to flip, where R solves p·e−R·win + q·eR·loss = 1. That pins the long-run probability within a narrow band, and the band gets tighter the more flips are computed.
  • Certainty equivalent uses CRRA utility u(W) = W1−γ/(1−γ) (log when γ = 1). At γ = 1, betting this flip is Kelly-optimal when the $1,000 stake is 2.38% of your wealth.
